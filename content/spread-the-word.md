Open Blues has no marketing, no budget and nobody whose job this is. It reaches people
because someone who is going says something to someone who isn't yet. That is the whole
machine, and this page is the toolkit for it.

It also has a floor: the gathering goes ahead only once **{{< param threshold >}} people have paid the
€50 Reservation Payment** by {{< param goNoGoHuman >}}. Below that it is cancelled and everyone is
refunded. So telling your local scene about it is not promotion. It is the thing that makes
the gathering exist.

## The print kit

Small cards you can carry in a jacket pocket and hand to someone at a blues night, and a
poster for a studio wall. Each one carries a QR code to this website and almost nothing
else — deliberately. The card's only job is to make someone curious enough to scan it. The
website does all the explaining.

Print them at home. There is nothing to order and nothing to pay for.

{{< print-kit >}}

<div class="print-note">
<strong>Printing:</strong> choose <em>Actual size</em> or <em>100%</em> rather than "fit to page",
print single-sided on ordinary paper (anything from 100&nbsp;g upwards feels better in the hand),
and cut along the faint guides. One A4 sheet gives you eight pocket cards or four postcards.
Every sheet is black ink only, so it is cheap to run.
</div>

## Who to hand them to

Hand these to people you have actually danced with — one at a time, face to face, never a
stack left on the bar. Tell them you are going yourself. The card only has to get them as far
as this page; you can answer everything else standing right there.

<ul class="handout">
<li>The people in your regular class you always end up talking to afterwards.</li>
<li>The friend who cooks for eight without being asked.</li>
<li>Someone a year into dancing who has never been to a festival.</li>
<li>The person you would want in the kitchen at three in the morning.</li>
<li>Whoever runs your local blues night — ask them first, then leave a few by the door.</li>
</ul>

Be honest about who it is not for. Five days of cooking, cleaning and sleeping on floors is
not everyone's idea of a holiday, and it is better to say so now than to have someone arrive
expecting a hotel. Open Blues is a private gathering of about forty people, and it works
because most of the room was invited by someone who knew them.

## What to say about it

If someone asks you what it actually is, the honest version is more attractive than the
polished one — and it filters for the people who will love it.

**Say:** it is private and cost-shared, about forty people; everyone cooks, cleans and runs
the workshops; nobody is paid and nobody profits; the whole five days costs somewhere around
€130–205 all in; you sleep in a shared bed, on a mattress, or in a tent in the park; it only
happens if forty people commit.

**Don't say:** festival, all-inclusive, luxury, teachers, or anything that implies somebody
will be looking after you. Do not promise anyone a place — there is a limit and the signups
close on {{< param closeHuman >}}. Nobody should arrive expecting a hotel in a castle.
